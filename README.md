Ralph Cifaretto is a capo in the DiMeo crime family of North Jersey in The Sopranos. Despite being "born handsome instead of rich," he is a smart, snappy-dressing top earner with violent, psychopathic tendencies. As the series progresses, he comes to be reviled by virtually every character in the show as well as most viewers. He meets his end when he comes to blows with Tony, the head of the family, after Pie-O-My—the racehorse they co-own—dies mysteriously in a stable fire.

His surname, Cifaretto, caught my attention because of its resemblance to the benchmark computer vision dataset CIFAR-10. Since horse is one of the ten class labels in CIFAR-10, I thought it would be a fitting experiment to use adversarial learning to force a model to misclassify an image of Ralphie as a horse, given the role the animal plays in his downfall.This repository explores how Differential Evolution can be employed as a black-box attack to generate image perturbations without requiring gradient information—relying solely on the full probability vector output by the target model.

Starting with a model trained on CIFAR-10, the pipeline scales up in model complexity and input resolution to attack architectures trained on CIFAR-100 and eventually ImageNet (1000 classes). As the resolution increases from $32 \times 32$ to $224 \times 224$ and the class space expands, the pixel budget (the number of perturbed pixels) required for a successful attack increases accordingly. For datasets where an explicit horse class is absent, target classes were chosen based on proximity to equine features—such as camel for CIFAR-100 and sorrel for ImageNet.
